## Understanding the challenge

We appear to have a public key and a message. Decrypting the message should give us the flag.

### How does RSA encryption work?

RSA encryption uses very large prime numbers. It relies on a calculation being easy in one direction but extremely difficult in reverse. For example, if you were told that 701,111 is the product of two prime numbers, could you work out which two numbers they are? Even with a calculator or computer, most of us would not know where to begin, let alone find the answer. Turning the calculation around makes it much easier.

### Key generation

RSA keys are generated as follows:

* Choose two prime numbers, p and q.
    * For security, p and q should be selected randomly. They should be similar in size, but differ in length by a few digits to make factoring harder.
* Calculate n = pq.
    * n is used as the modulus for both the public and private keys.

## Decryption

We will use RsaCtfTool to decrypt the message. First, clone the tool:

```shell
git clone https://github.com/Ganapati/RsaCtfTool
```

Put the encrypted flag, the public key, and the tool in the same directory, as shown below.

![Desktop view](/assets/img/sample/HTC/Weak-rsa_rsatool.png)

Run:

```shell
./RsaCtfTool/RsaCtfTool.py --publickey key.pub --uncipherfile flag.enc
```

This gives us the flag.
