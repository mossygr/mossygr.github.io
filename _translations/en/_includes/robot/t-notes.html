Detailed connection notes

This is the reference for checking or changing a particular connection. You do not need to memorise it. Return to the relevant section with the diagram and table before making a change.

Battery safety

5A fuse and battery protection (recommended) The 3×18650 holder has no protection. A short circuit can heat a thin wire enough to start a fire. Fit a 5A inline fuse immediately after battery positive, before the switch. Use protected cells or a 3S BMS board.

Battery voltage Three 18650 cells in series provide 9.0V empty to 12.6V fully charged, with an 11.1V nominal voltage. The HW-468 converter needs at least 9V, so two 18650 cells are insufficient. Recharge before the cells fall below ~3.2V each, or ~9.6V overall.

Wires and WAGO connectors Use red/black 0.5mm² wire for the battery, switch, L298N, and converter. Dupont leads with female terminals do not fit into WAGO connectors: cut off the terminal and strip 11 mm. Because Dupont wire is very thin, below 0.14mm², tin it or use 0.5mm² wire for the section going to the WAGO.

Power and grounding

HW-468: only three screws IN+ · GND · OUT+. Input and output share GND, so battery ground reaches the converter through WAGO B → WAGO GND, wires #8 and #9. Screw order may vary: check the labels on the board.

Common ground All grounds are connected: battery, L298N, converter, ESP32-CAM, DFPlayer, and amplifier. Motor current returns directly to the battery through WAGO B rather than through the logic WAGO GND.

470–1000µF / 16V capacitor Solder it beside the ESP32-CAM's 5V and GND pins, onto wires #11 and #12. The long positive leg goes to 5V; the side with the negative stripe goes to GND. Reversing it may make it burst. Never put it on the 11.1V supply: the guide treats a 16V rating as marginal there. It helps prevent brown-out resets when Wi-Fi transmits or the flash turns on.

ESP32-CAM

Programming with the ESP32-CAM-MB base The MB base is used only at the desk. Remove the ESP32-CAM from the robot's wiring, plug it into the MB, and connect USB. Never connect USB and robot battery power simultaneously: both supplies would be connected to the same 5V rail. After the first upload, later upgrades can use Wi-Fi OTA without unscrewing anything.

If uploading fails Only if programming with the board still connected to the robot wiring, which is not recommended: temporarily disconnect IO2, wire #21. IO2 must be LOW or disconnected to enter download mode. If it stalls at “Connecting…”, hold IO0 on the MB, press RST on the ESP32-CAM, then release IO0.

IO12 and a 10kΩ resistor (optional, recommended) IO12 selects the flash-memory voltage during startup. If it is HIGH at reset, the board will not boot. It normally remains LOW; a 10kΩ resistor from IO12 to GND, #35 and #36, ensures this. Never add a pull-up to IN4.

ESP32-CAM ground Use the GND beside 5V. On some clones, the last pin on the other row is labelled “GND/R” and is not a reliable ground.

The light is the board's flash The white LED is on GPIO4 and needs no wire. GPIO4 is also an SD-card line, which is why the SD card is not used.

Symptoms

Reset or disconnection when movement starts or the light turns on This points to a voltage drop, or brown-out. In order: (1) charge the cells—below ~3.2V per cell, a stalled motor can pull the supply below the converter's 9V minimum; (2) check C1 is fitted with the correct polarity; (3) shorten or thicken #11/#12.

The camera picture freezes The page detects this and reconnects automatically within ~3 s. The stream serves one device at a time: close it on other phones or tabs.

L298N and motors

L298N jumpers and 5V Leave ENA, ENB, and 5V-EN fitted. Leave the L298N 5V terminal unconnected: it is an output, so do not connect it to the converter's 5V. The firmware controls speed with PWM on IN1–IN4. Terminal order may vary: check the board's printed labels.

TT motors are rated for 3–6V With a full battery, the L298N supplies ~10V. The firmware therefore limits PWM to ~65%, approximately a 6–7V average, and starts at ~30%. Do not raise MAX_DUTY above 170 without a reason: the motors heat up and the gears wear.

Two motors per channel Connect the two left motors in parallel to OUT1/OUT2 and the two right motors to OUT3/OUT4, positive to positive and negative to negative. Each screw takes two wires; twist them together before tightening. If one motor runs opposite to the other on its side, swap the two wires on that motor only. If a whole side runs backwards, change INVERT_LEFT/INVERT_RIGHT in robot_config.h.

Current and temperature Two TT motors together can draw more than 2A if stalled. The firmware ramps speed up gradually. Leave room around the L298N heatsink; if it gets too hot, it shuts down temporarily.

A twitch at startup Some ESP32-CAM pins briefly toggle during power-on. The firmware sets them LOW immediately, but the motors may move for a fraction of a second. This is expected.

Audio

DFPlayer Pro (DFR0768) Only VIN, GND, RX, and DACL are connected. Leave L+/L−/R+/R−, PLAY, KEY, DACR, and TX unconnected. GND is required. Pin order follows DFRobot's numbering: check the labels on the board.

Only the left channel is heard Only DACL → LIN is connected; DACR → RIN was removed because there is one speaker. Make MP3s mono or mix both channels together, otherwise anything present only on the right channel will be lost.

DFR0119 amplifier Its two GND pins are connected internally, so one is enough. SHDN needs no wire: an internal pull-up keeps the amplifier on; LOW turns it off. Leave RIN, R+, and R− unconnected. Supply voltage must not exceed 5.5V. For pin order, check the printed labels.

Speaker Connect only the amplifier's L+ and L− to the speaker. Never connect L− to GND or join L− and R−. Keep the volume around 15–25 out of 30 to avoid distortion.
